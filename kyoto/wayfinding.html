How the backend works

1. The system has already tagged every photo with who's in it and when it was taken

Before you ever opened the search, the system had already scanned every photo in your library and recorded things like who appears in it and roughly when it was taken. This happens continuously in the background, not just when you search.

For this trip, three photos were tagged as containing Ken and Maya, all from a window in Summer 2022.

2. You gave two clues: who you were with, and roughly when

You tapped Ken & Maya and Summer 2022. The system compared these two clues against every photo in your library, not just the three from this trip.

3. Photos that match both clues rise to the top; everything else falls away naturally

Thousands of other photos, food, documents, unrelated trips, don't contain Ken or Maya and weren't taken in that window, so they score far too low to matter. They aren't deleted or blocked, they just don't come close to matching.

The three photos that do show Ken and Maya, in that summer, rise above everything else, because they match on both of your clues at once.

4. Among those three, the clearest, strongest match comes first

All three show Ken and Maya in the right season. The one where both of them are most clearly recognizable becomes the top result, the other two sit right beside it as close, related options, since they're clearly from the same moment.

The result: two simple clues, who and roughly when, were enough to narrow thousands of photos down to the three that mattered, and surface the best one first.
