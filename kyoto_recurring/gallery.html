How the backend works

1. The system has already tagged every photo with who’s in it and what objects appear in it

Before you ever searched, the system had already scanned every photo and recorded things like who appears in it and what objects or items are visible, a diya, a rangoli, and so on. This happens continuously in the background.

Two relevant photos exist here: one from 2023, tagged with your sister and a diya. One from 2024, tagged with a rangoli and a group that includes your sister.

2. You gave two clues: who you were with, and an object you remembered

You tapped Sister and Rangoli. The system compared both clues against your tagged photos.

3. One clue was wrong, but the system didn’t throw the photo out because of it

The 2023 photo matches on “Sister” but doesn’t match “Rangoli,” it actually has a diya, not a rangoli. A system that required every clue to match perfectly would have excluded this photo entirely because of that one wrong detail.

Instead, the system weighs clues rather than requiring all of them to match. “Sister” is a strong, specific match. One mismatched object isn’t enough to override that.

4. The 2023 photo still comes out on top, with the 2024 photo offered as a close second

The 2023 photo ranks highest overall, because matching “Sister” closely outweighs the one mismatch on “Rangoli.” The 2024 photo, which does have a rangoli and does include your sister in a group shot, is surfaced right alongside it as a reasonable second option, since it’s not an unreasonable guess, just not the strongest match.

The result: even with one confidently wrong detail, the system still found the right photo, because it weighs several clues together instead of demanding a perfect match on every one.

Case 2: Diwali recurring

What you remember

“Diwali, some year recently. I was with my sister, I think there was a rangoli, but honestly every year looks the same to me.”

How the memory gets rebuilt
  1. Scroll the gallery to fire the prompt, or tap the compass icon.
  2. Who → tap Sister.
  3. Objects or details visible → tap Rangoli. The memory is wrong; that is the point.
  4. Tap Launch Wayfinding Stream: the deck ranks Diwali 2023 strongest. Sister is a specific match; rangoli matches 2024 instead.
  5. Tap the 2023 candidate: the grid shows only that year’s own photos, diya imagery, zero rangoli. The stream routes to the blue-dress photo.
  6. Confirm screen shows Attire-confirmed.

Demonstrates: a vague memory with one wrong, confidently-stated detail still resolves correctly. The system weighs multiple partial cues; it does not require every one to match.
