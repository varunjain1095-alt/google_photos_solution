How the backend works

1. The system has already scanned the text and visible details on every document photo

Before you ever searched, the system had already scanned every screenshot and document photo in your library, reading any printed text, dates, and visual details, like whether a page shows a signature, a stamp, or a prescription format. This happens continuously in the background.

For this item, the system had already recorded: text matching a prescription format, a printed date, and that it wasn’t a photo you personally took, it arrived from someone else.

2. You gave a few clues: the type of item, whether it had a date, and where it came from

You tapped Prescription/Rx, Had a date printed on it, and Received from someone else. The system compared these against every document-type photo in your library.

3. Only items matching this combination rise to the top

Photos that aren’t prescriptions, don’t have a printed date, or were photos you took yourself don’t match this combination and fall away. The prescription from Dr. Sharma’s clinic matches all three clues, it’s tagged as a prescription, it has a printed date, and it was received, not self-captured.

4. Related scans from the same document set are surfaced alongside it

Because documents are often part of a set, a prescription page, a receipt, a lab report, from the same visit, the system also surfaces those nearby related scans, so you can see the whole document set, not just the one match.

The result: three clues about what kind of document it was, not who was in it or where it happened, were enough to find the exact document, along with the related paperwork from that same visit.

What you remember

“Someone sent me a photo of a prescription. Sharma clinic? Sometime this year. I need it for the pharmacy and I never saved it anywhere.”

How the memory gets rebuilt
  1. Scroll the gallery to fire the prompt, or tap the compass icon.
  2. Tap the Utility / Quick Find tab at the top of the drawer.
  3. Documents & Screenshots → tap Prescription / Rx.
  4. Visual & Material Clues → tap Had a date printed on it.
  5. Date Horizon → tap 2024.
  6. Entity & Origin → tap Received from someone else.
  7. The deck shows 3 candidate document spaces. Tap the primary, Dr. Sharma Clinic Prescription & Medical Bill: the prescription document, Text-matched, with nearby scans beside it.

Demonstrates: the no-event branch. Standalone items resolve through document facets (printed text, received-vs-captured, revisit), not event streams.
